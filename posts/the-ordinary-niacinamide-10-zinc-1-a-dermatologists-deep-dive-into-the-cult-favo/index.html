The Ordinary Niacinamide 10% + Zinc 1%: A Dermatologist's Deep Dive into the Cult-Favorite Serum

2026-07-21 · Product Reviews · Tags: The, product reviews, skincarescience

Few skincare products have achieved the cult status of The Ordinary's Niacinamide 10% + Zinc 1% serum. It's affordable, widely available, and promises to tackle everything from enlarged pores to uneven texture. But as a dermatologist, I've learned that hype and science don't always align. In this review, I'll break down the formulation, the evidence behind its key ingredients, and who should—and shouldn't—add this serum to their routine. By the end, you'll know exactly whether this $6 bottle deserves a spot on your shelf.

Understanding Niacinamide: The Multi-Tasking Molecule

Niacinamide, also known as vitamin B3, is one of the most researched skincare ingredients in dermatology. Its versatility is remarkable: it supports the skin barrier by boosting ceramide synthesis, reduces inflammation, regulates sebum production, and even helps fade hyperpigmentation by inhibiting melanosome transfer. At concentrations of 2% to 5%, niacinamide has been shown to improve skin elasticity and reduce fine lines. The Ordinary's serum uses a 10% concentration—a level that is effective but also more likely to cause irritation in sensitive individuals.

The addition of zinc (as zinc PCA) is intriguing. Zinc is known for its anti-inflammatory and sebum-regulating properties. In theory, combining it with niacinamide creates a powerful duo for oily, acne-prone skin. However, the concentration of zinc in this formula is not disclosed, and the clinical evidence for zinc PCA in topical skincare is less robust than for niacinamide itself. Still, the combination has become iconic, and many users report noticeable improvements in oil control and breakouts.

Deconstructing the Formulation: More Than Just Actives

A product is more than its star ingredients. Let's look at the full ingredient list: Aqua (Water), Niacinamide, Pentylene Glycol, Zinc PCA, Tamarindus Indica Seed Gum, Xanthan Gum, Isoceteth-20, Ethoxydiglycol, Phenoxyethanol, Chlorphenesin.

The formula is surprisingly simple. Pentylene glycol acts as a humectant and preservative booster, while tamarind seed gum and xanthan gum provide a slightly viscous texture. Ethoxydiglycol is a penetration enhancer, which helps niacinamide absorb deeper into the skin. The preservatives (phenoxyethanol and chlorphenesin) are safe and well-tolerated. Notably, there are no fragrances, essential oils, or drying alcohols—a plus for sensitive skin.

The texture is a lightweight gel-like serum that spreads easily and dries down to a slightly tacky finish. This tackiness can be an issue under makeup or heavier moisturizers, but it's a minor trade-off for the affordability. The pH of the formula is around 5.5–6.0, which is compatible with the skin's natural acid mantle, minimizing the risk of barrier disruption.

Who Should Use This Serum? (And Who Shouldn't)

This serum is designed for individuals with oily, combination, or acne-prone skin. The 10% niacinamide concentration is effective for reducing sebum production and refining the appearance of pores. If you struggle with blackheads, whiteheads, or frequent breakouts, this product can be a valuable addition to your routine.

However, if you have dry or highly sensitive skin, proceed with caution. Ten percent niacinamide is significantly higher than the typical 2–5% used in most clinical studies. In my practice, I've seen patients experience flushing, stinging, and contact dermatitis from high-concentration niacinamide. If you're new to niacinamide, start with a lower percentage (e.g., 5%) or use this serum only twice a week to build tolerance. Also, if you are using other active ingredients like retinol or exfoliating acids, layering them with this serum may increase irritation. Always patch test for at least 48 hours.

Real-World Performance: What Results Can You Expect?

In my clinical experience, niacinamide at 10% can produce visible improvements in oil control within 2–4 weeks. Many users report a matte finish that lasts several hours, and a reduction in the frequency of breakouts. The zinc component may also help calm active inflammation, making pimples less red and swollen.

For hyperpigmentation and post-acne marks, niacinamide works more slowly—expect 8–12 weeks of consistent use before seeing a noticeable lightening. It's not as potent as hydroquinone or prescription retinoids, but it's a gentle alternative for those who prefer over-the-counter options. Importantly, this serum does not cause the “purging” that you might experience with retinoids or acids. Instead, any initial breakouts are likely due to irritation or an allergic reaction, which should prompt you to discontinue use.

One common complaint is that the serum feels sticky or leaves a film. This is due to the high concentration of niacinamide and the gum-based thickeners. To mitigate this, apply it to damp skin or mix a drop with your moisturizer. You can also follow with a facial mist to help it absorb better.

How to Incorporate It into Your Routine

Placement matters. Niacinamide is water-soluble and works best when applied to clean, dry skin before heavier creams. In the morning, use it after cleansing and before vitamin C (if you use it) or sunscreen. In the evening, it can be used after cleansing and before retinol—but if you're new to either, alternate them to avoid overloading your skin.

Here are my top tips for using this serum effectively:

  • Start with a pea-sized amount for your entire face—more is not better.
  • Apply it to slightly damp skin to improve spreadability and reduce tackiness.
  • Wait 30–60 seconds for it to dry before layering other products.
  • If you have sensitive skin, use it only at night and rinse off in the morning.
  • Always finish with a moisturizer and broad-spectrum SPF during the day, as niacinamide can increase photosensitivity in some individuals.

Avoid using this serum in the same routine as strong acids (like glycolic or salicylic acid) or high-dose vitamin C (above 15%). While niacinamide and vitamin C are not inherently incompatible, the high pH of this serum can destabilize certain vitamin C forms. If you must use both, apply vitamin C in the morning and niacinamide at night.

Comparing with Other Niacinamide Serums

The Ordinary's offering is not the only niacinamide serum on the market. Paula's Choice 10% Niacinamide Booster offers a similar concentration but includes additional soothing ingredients like licorice root extract and panthenol. It's more expensive but may be better tolerated. Another option is Naturium's Niacinamide Serum 12% Plus Zinc 2%, which uses a higher zinc concentration and a more elegant texture. However, The Ordinary remains the most budget-friendly and has a proven track record for efficacy.

In my dermatological opinion, the differences between these products are marginal. The key is to find a formulation that your skin tolerates and that fits your texture preferences. If you have dry skin, you might prefer a niacinamide cream or lotion instead of a serum. If you have oily skin, this serum is a great starting point.

Final Verdict: Is It Worth the Hype?

As a dermatologist, I appreciate The Ordinary's transparency and commitment to evidence-based ingredients. This serum delivers what it promises: oil control, pore refinement, and a reduction in breakouts for many users. The price point is unbeatable, and the simple formulation minimizes the risk of irritation from unnecessary additives.

That said, it's not a miracle cure. The 10% concentration may be too harsh for some, and the texture can be off-putting. If you're new to niacinamide, I recommend starting with a lower percentage or using this product sparingly. For those with oily, acne-prone skin who can tolerate high concentrations, this serum is a solid, cost-effective choice. It's not the most luxurious product, but it's one of the most scientifically sound. In my practice, I've seen it transform stubborn oily skin—when used correctly.

So, is it worth the hype? For the right skin type, absolutely. For everyone else, there are gentler options. But if you're willing to listen to your skin and adjust accordingly, this cult favorite earns its place in many routines.

Conclusion

The Ordinary Niacinamide 10% + Zinc 1% serum is a testament to the power of simplicity. It combines two well-researched ingredients at effective concentrations, all for the price of a coffee. While it's not suitable for everyone, those with oily, blemish-prone skin will likely see significant benefits with consistent use. As with any active, start slow, patch test, and listen to your skin. If you do, this serum could be the oil-control, pore-refining hero you've been searching for.
